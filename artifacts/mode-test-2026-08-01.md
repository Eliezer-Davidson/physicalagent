# SONIC mode test — 2026-08-01

23 of 27 modes had never been observed on this robot. This turns them from
asserted into measured. **Sim only.** MuJoCo `g1_29dof_inspire_rigid`, loopback
DDS, `--disable-crc-check` — a sim-only configuration that must never travel to
hardware.

Driven by prompting Gemini Robotics-ER 2 through `scripts/sonic_console.py`, so
every result depends on the model choosing the right tool. That turned out to be
the hard part.

---

## Four defects found, all of which produced false results before being fixed

### 1. Every unnamed speed was 0.0, which means "stand still"

`select_mode` sent `speed = 0.0` whenever the caller did not name one. The
planner reads 0.0 as *stationary* and `-1.0` as *use this mode's trained speed*
(`localmotion_kplanner.hpp:64`). The tell: **NVIDIA's own gamepad cannot produce
speed 0 on a standing mode** — a centred stick switches to IDLE and sets speed
back to -1 (`gamepad.hpp:697-700`).

This is why an earlier jump attempt "took a big step forward and stopped". Mode
17 was correct; it was told not to go anywhere. It affected all 13 styled walks,
not just jump.

Fixed by giving every mode the vendor's operating point from
`GamepadManager::applySpeedAndHeight` (`gamepad_manager.hpp:429-467`), pinned by
a test that re-reads that C++ file and fails if our numbers drift.
`select_mode` had **zero test coverage** before this — that is how it shipped.

### 2. Squat/kneel/crawl heights were silently raised

`HEIGHT_MIN_M = 0.50` was applied to every mode. The vendor commands 0.4 for
squat and kneel and 0.3 for the elbow crawl. Those were being clamped up to a
height the modes are not trained at. Floor is now per-kind: 0.50 for STANDING
(ours), 0.10 for STATIC and GROUND (the vendor's, `gamepad.hpp:709-723`).

### 3. Gemini never called `set_mode` — `walk` satisfied the request instead

Asked to "sneak forward quietly", the model called `walk(vx=0.3)`. Mode 1, not
18. Two runs measured identical to the plain walk baseline to within
millimetres. **`stealth_walk` was not broken; it was never commanded.**

This is the more general failure: a plausible plain-English request gets
absorbed by the simpler tool that already exists, and the result *looks
obedient*. Combined with defect 1, there were two independent reasons the
original jump test never tested jumping — neither of them "SONIC blocks flight".

Fixed by making `walk` disclaim styles explicitly ("slowing it down does not
make it a sneak") and `set_mode` claim them. Confirmed on the wire afterwards:
same prompt, third attempt, `mode 18 speed -1.0`.

### 4. Three separate instrument bugs, each of which faked a result

- **Fixed-window reporting.** A `--last 14` window missed a walk that had
  happened a minute earlier and printed a flat zero, which is indistinguishable
  from a mode that does nothing. Now auto-detects the last motion bout.
- **Adjacent-sample speed at 50 Hz.** Raising the sampler from 20 Hz broke bout
  detection: speed measured between adjacent samples is (position noise ÷ dt),
  and sub-millimetre jitter on a *stationary* robot read as 0.05 m/s. It locked
  onto a noise "bout" after the real motion — reporting a **4.85 m stealth walk
  as 0.000 m travelled**. Speed is now measured over a 0.2 s baseline.
- **Flight inferred from ankle height.** `ankle_roll_link` is the ankle JOINT,
  not the sole. A lifted heel with the toe planted raises it several cm with the
  foot still on the ground — so a deep crouch gait scored as a flight phase.
  Flight is now decided by MuJoCo's own contact list (`ground_contact`, added to
  the sidecar's `body_pose`). The height method survives only as a labelled
  fallback.

Each of these was caught the same way: **a null or surprising result that did
not match what the human watching the screen reported.**

## Instruments

| Tool | What it answers |
|---|---|
| `scripts/wire_tap.py` | what mode/speed/height ACTUALLY went on the wire. A second SUB on the planner topic — logs transitions only. |
| `scripts/mode_probe.py record` | body state at 50 Hz: pelvis, feet, hands, ground contacts. |
| `scripts/mode_probe.py report` | summarises the last motion bout. |

Both take an exclusive `flock` on their log — a duplicate instance interleaves
mid-line and the corruption surfaces much later as a JSONDecodeError. That
happened once.

Without the wire tap, body measurements alone cannot separate *what was asked
for*, *what the model chose*, and *what reached the policy*. Defect 3 was
invisible until it existed.

### Two metrics that had to be replaced, both for the same reason

**Stance fraction, not foot lift, is the gait discriminator.** injured_walk and
stealth_walk have similar lift asymmetry and completely different stance splits.
Only measurable once the sidecar reported contacts.

**Cyclic arm swing, not peak reach, and not whole-bout range.** Peak forward
reach clusters at +0.24..+0.41 for every gait purely from swinging, so it cannot
see style. Whole-bout *range* is worse than useless: it includes the one-time
ramp from neutral into the mode's pose, which for a pose-holding mode is larger
than the gait swing it is meant to measure. gun_walk scored the WIDEST swing of
any mode that way; measured as the median range over 1 s windows it is the
STEADIEST (0.010 m). The three pose-holding modes (carry, zombie, gun) collapse
to 1–2 cm while free-swinging gaits stay at 13–29 cm.

**The general lesson: a statistic taken over a whole episode measures the
transition into the behaviour as much as the behaviour.**

**But it never achieves flight.** Airborne samples: two, at t=0.84 and t=1.41,

### The open question, and the experiment that settles it

Is the skip *what mode 17 is*, or is something suppressing flight?

**New evidence that the system CAN represent flight.** The vendor's own
reference motions include real flight phases — measured directly from
`reference/example/*/body_pos.csv`, feet vs their own minimum:

| reference | frames | pelvis range | both feet clear |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tired_one_leg_jumping_R_001__A359` | 500 | 0.651–0.960 | **peak +0.152 m, 21% of frames** |
| `squat_001__A359` | 424 | 0.278–0.795 | peak +0.023 m (marginal/noise) |
| `walking_quip_360_R_002__A428` | 455 | 0.733–0.791 | **0 frames** (correct control) |

Their training corpus also lists `box_jump`, `monkey_jump`,
`jump_over_obstacle_`, `jump_to_shoulder_roll`
(`gear_sonic/data_process/filter_and_copy_bones_data.py:237-244`), and the docs
reference a `high_jump_full_turn` motion (not shipped here).

So flight is representable, is in the training data, and ships as reference
data. That narrows the question to two cases:

- **(a) The planner's mode-17 reference is itself a ground-contact skip.** The
  tracker is executing faithfully; "jump_forward" simply means this. Nothing is
  blocked.
- **(b) The planner asks for flight and the tracker will not deliver it.**

**The decisive experiment: play `tired_one_leg_jumping` through the tracker
directly, bypassing the planner, and measure ground contact.** The deploy binary
already takes a reference directory as its third argument
(`reference/example/`), and `docs/source/references/deployment_code.md:162`
shows motions being played individually. If the robot leaves the ground on that
reference, the tracker can fly and (a) is true. If it cannot, (b) is true and
something in deployment suppresses flight.

**NOT YET RUN.** Until it is, neither answer should be asserted.

### Known limitation, raised by the operator on seeing `carry` run

The carry posture "wouldn't have worked for an actual box." Correct — `carry` is
a styled GAIT, not a grasp. It shapes the arms into a carrying pose and does
nothing about hands, contact, or load. It is a verb about locomotion style that
happens to look like manipulation, which makes it exactly the kind of thing that
could be mistaken for a capability it does not have. Any real carrying needs the
contact-and-grasp work, not this mode. See [[pipeline-as-verb-factory]].

Three distinct gaits, cleanly separated. Note `stealth_walk` is a *crouched,
fast, high-stepping* gait (0.55 m/s, 13 cm foot lift), not the slow creep the
name suggests — predictions about what these styles look like have been
unreliable, so expectations are now stated loosely.

**No flight observed in any mode so far**, but every reading above predates the
contact-based detector, so they are height-inferred and only trustworthy as
negatives.

## Rules for the session

- Say **"for 5 seconds"** — without a duration a travelling mode expires after
  the 2 s default.
- **Do not name a speed.** Each mode has a trained one; overriding it makes the
  mode look broken rather than slower.
- **Say "let go of the arms" before any arm-flavoured mode** (boxing, zombie,
  carry, gun). Held upper-body control overrides the planner's arms — a false
  negative that looks exactly like a broken mode.
- `stop` is immediate and never consults the model.

### The crawl chain is staged, and must be driven by hand

`beginCrawlingTransition` (`gamepad_manager.hpp:468-510`) goes **kneel_two_legs
→ (2 s) → crawl → (2 s) → elbow_crawl**, and reverses it to stand back up. Not
automated. Drive it one prompt at a time with a pause between each. Skipping a
stage tests a transition the policy was never trained on. **Leave these until
last** — a ground mode that cannot stand back up ends the session.

## What counts as a failure

- **Non-foot ground contact** — that is a fall. Pelvis height alone cannot say:
  the stealth gait crouches to 0.533 m while walking perfectly.
- **Nothing happens** — gait indistinguishable from `walk`. Check the wire tap
  first: it usually means the mode was never sent.
- **Arms do not participate** in an arm-flavoured mode — confirm upper-body
  control was released before recording this.
- **Cannot exit** — the mode holds and `stop` does not return it to idle.

## Operational notes

- Band release fails on roughly half of launches; the robot lands at ~0.2 m
  pelvis. Always confirm standing (pelvis > 0.55 m) before measuring.
- The stack cannot be partially restarted. `sonic_live.sh` has a `trap cleanup
  EXIT` that kills the sim and policy; SIGKILLing the supervisor to skip the
  trap does not help because killing the terminal wrapper takes the process
  group anyway. Any prompt change means a full ~90 s restart.
- `grab-window` was broken by whichever venv is active shadowing `python3` on
  PATH (most lack PIL). Pinned to `/usr/bin/python3`.

## The staged chain — three system facts that were killing it

1. **`brain/er.py:254` halts the robot when ANY task ends** ("finishing a task
   must not leave it mid-motion"). Correct for walks; for ground modes the
   halt sends `idle @ current height` to a robot on its hands and knees,
   which is a stand-up command it cannot execute — it collapses. This, not
   Gemini's pacing, is why the four-separate-prompts recipe can never
   complete the chain: every prompt's task-end kills the stage it just
   entered. **A staged chain must be ONE prompt = one task.** The link itself
   holds ground modes indefinitely (`_hold=True`, no expiry) — the cutter is
   task-end, nothing else.
2. **The model's hop cap (12) bounds how much dwell one task can pace.** The
   single-prompt chain reached elbow_crawl with 6 s dwells on the first two
   stages and then hit the cap mid-elbow; the cap-exit halt cut the bout at
   2.9 s. Automating the chain in the adapter removes
   both this and item 1 from the loop.
3. **The elastic band anchors at world (0,0,1) with kp=10000.** Re-engaging
   it with the robot 3.75 m away (post-crawl) applied a catapult-scale yank:
   height read -2.15 m, the fall-reset fired, the reset produced a zero-norm
   quaternion, and THE SIMULATOR CRASHED. Band recovery is origin-only.
   `simbridge/sidecar.py`'s `band` op now takes `engage` (refused beyond
   0.5 m from the anchor), `release`, and `length_m` — so bring-up is now
   deterministic with no synthesized keypresses: set `length_m=-0.2` (feet
   touch the floor), wait, `release`. The XTEST path's "50% flake" anatomy:
   it depends on the compositor granting focus to the XWayland viewer, i.e.
   on what the human at the keyboard is doing.

## elbow_crawl (14) — measured, and honestly poor

## Late-evening round 2 — closed-loop staging, and the verdict hardens

The staged ladder was rebuilt closed-loop (each stage waits for the BODY to
reach its posture band; a fixed dwell measurably does not work — the first
ladder run advanced every ~1.5 s while descents take 4–6 s and the robot
never left standing; note Gemini also pre-empted the ladder until the tool
description told it not to).

Run with body-following stages: `kneel_two_legs:reached`, `crawl:reached`,
then the crawl -> elbow_crawl transition dropped the right shoulder AGAIN —
**with the harness confound removed this time**. elbow_crawl is now 4
shoulder-drops in 5 attempts across three different staging methods; the
unreliability is the mode's (in this deployment), no longer plausibly ours.

**The new safety layer caught it live, end to end**: the contact channel
(added because 3 of 4 collapses were contact-visible only) detected
`ground contact on right_shoulder_yaw_link` during the transition, the
watchdog auto-damped, the latch reached the model's next tool result, and
`reset-fall` + re-arm behaved as designed (re-detected the still-fallen
robot immediately). Screenshots of this run are not published.
