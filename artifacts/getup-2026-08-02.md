# Can SONIC's mode vocabulary get the G1 off the floor? — measured 2026-08-02

The question: can a reasoning agent get the G1 up using only SONIC's existing modes, with no
new verbs — reason its way to the right sequence of poses SONIC already allows?

That is an empirical claim about the ACTION SPACE, not about reasoning: if some sequence of
the 27 modes stands a fallen robot up, a reasoning agent can find it; if none does, no
cleverness will. So it was measured. Harness: `scripts/getup_probe.py` as `SONIC_FRONTEND`
under `scripts/sonic_live.sh`, `SONIC_FALL_RESET_M=0.05`, wire tap recording the commanded
side, sidecar (MuJoCo's own state) recording the measured side at 10–50 Hz, screenshots at
every phase boundary. Logs: `artifacts/getup/run{2,3,4}_session.log` + matching
`*_getup_log.jsonl` (run 1 was void — see "harness bug" below — and was discarded).

STANDING = pelvis >= 0.70 m AND only feet touching the floor AND feet actually ON the
floor, sustained 3 s. Idle stands at 0.786; the hanging robot reads 0.96 with no contacts,
which is why all three clauses exist.

## What this settles

1. **From a DELIBERATE lie-down, the mode vocabulary CAN stand the robot up** — first
   demonstration ever here, two distinct routes (kneel ladder; crawl route). The reverse
   ladder that was "undemonstrated" as of last night is now demonstrated, from a clean
   (twisted-prone) lie at ~0.15 m.
2. **The obvious move is a trap.** `idle` from prone — the first thing a reasoning agent
   tries, "just stand" — does not merely fail: it thrashes, drags the robot metres, and in
   sim ends the session (on hardware that flailing would be violent). The action space
   contains both working routes and catastrophic ones with nothing in the interface to
   tell them apart; that knowledge exists only as measurement.
3. **Why crawl_route works:** the crawl reference itself lifts a prone body into a 0.449 m
   quadruped (measured, speed 0). The modes are postures WITH entry transitions; the
   transitions are the hidden capability.
4. **Recovery from a GENUINE fall posture works (n=1)** — and the recipe matters:
   `lie_face_down` FIRST. Commanded on the twisted fall (shoulder+elbow down, 0.225 m) it
   NORMALISED the body into the clean prone the ascent routes are known to work from
   (0.151 m), and the crawl→kneel→idle ascent then stood it up. lie-as-normaliser is the
   step a naive agent would skip — "already on the floor, why lie down more?"
5. **jump_forward does not jump** at its vendor operating point (0/438 airborne samples in
   8 s). The "ability to actually jump" does not exist in SONIC-as-shipped here; a real
   jump verb would have to come from the streamed-reference channel or a new policy.

## What this does NOT settle

- One lie posture per attempt (the deliberate lie lands in the same twisted prone each
  time). Other fall geometries (supine, side-sprawl, legs-folded) are unmeasured.
- n=1 per route per run. The routes worked on their first try, but no reliability claim
  (x/10) is made.
- Speed-0 pinning appearing to stabilise the elbow_crawl transition is n=2 vs n=5
  across different harnesses — a lead to measure, not a fact.

## Harness bug that voided run 1 (and its fix)

`sonic_console.release_band()` verified the band release by tailing `SONIC_POSE_LOG.*` —
lexicographic sort picked a PREVIOUS session's pose log whose last line read 0.79 m, and
declared a robot hanging at 0.962 m "already free". Three "attempts" ran against a robot
flailing in mid-air before the sidecar mismatch was caught (seen live by the operator). Fixes
applied: `_pelvis_height` now picks newest-by-mtime and refuses files older than 30 s
(stale = a previous session, returns None → deterministic release-file path);
`getup_probe.standing_now` requires foot ground-contact, catching the hanging robot's
third failure mode (tall AND touching nothing).
